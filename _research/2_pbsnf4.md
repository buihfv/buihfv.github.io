<div class="media-text" markdown="1">
Inverse design can propose a composition, but a proposal that is never made in a furnace stays a proposal. This project is the group's first experimental workflow, and its purpose is to make the loop close: calculate, synthesize, measure, and feed the measurement back.

β-PbSnF₄ is the benchmark rather than the goal. It is the best-characterised fluoride-ion conductor available, so its ionic conductivity is a known number — which makes it the right material to test a workflow against. If the sample we make reproduces the reference value, the route and the measurement are trustworthy enough to use on a composition nobody has made before.

<div class="media-text" markdown="1">
Fluoride-ion batteries are attractive for the same reason they are difficult: F⁻ is small and singly charged, which gives a high theoretical energy density, but room-temperature conductors are scarce. That scarcity is exactly the gap inverse design is meant to fill — searching composition space for a structure that should conduct, rather than waiting for one to be found.
