I am an undergraduate researcher in the Materials Design and Process Engineering Laboratory at Sogang University, advised by Prof. Heonjae Jeong. I use first-principles calculations — DFT, ab initio molecular dynamics, and hybrid functionals — to explain why materials behave the way they do at the atomic scale, and to turn that explanation into something a process or device engineer can act on.

Two questions occupy me right now. The first is what happens at the buried interfaces of a thermal battery: how a garnet solid electrolyte survives contact with a lithium-silicon anode and an FeF₃ cathode at 500 °C, and which dopant buys both thermodynamic stability and fast Li-ion transport. The second is why an amorphous oxynitride semiconductor gets _less_ conductive under illumination — a defect-physics puzzle I worked through with AIMD and hybrid DFT.

I am applying to mechanical engineering PhD programs in the United States for 2027 enrollment.

## Research Interests

- First-principles modeling of solid–solid interfaces
- Solid electrolytes, thermal batteries, and all-solid-state cells
- Li-ion transport: migration barriers, AIMD, bond-valence site energy
- Defect physics and charge localization in amorphous semiconductors
- Machine-learning interatomic potentials and HPC workflow automation
