Hi! I am an undergraduate researcher in the Materials Design and Process Engineering Laboratory at Sogang University, advised by Prof. Heonjae Jeong. I use first-principles calculations — DFT, ab initio molecular dynamics and hybrid functionals — to explain why materials behave the way they do at the atomic scale, and to turn that explanation into something a process or device engineer can act on. It is very interesting!

My interest is thermal transport in semiconductor materials at the nanoscale. As devices scale down, heat stops being a by-product and becomes the binding constraint: it caps clock speed, accelerates degradation, and sets the limit on how densely a chip can be packed. I want to treat that as a materials problem rather than a packaging one — to understand how energy is carried and scattered inside the material itself, and to turn that understanding into materials that move heat where a device needs it to go. The approach I want to build my work on runs from prediction through to synthesis: first-principles calculations that establish a material's properties before anyone runs an experiment; inverse design to work in the other direction, starting from a target property and asking what structure would deliver it; machine-learning interatomic potentials to carry molecular dynamics into the many-atom, chemically complex systems that DFT alone cannot reach; and experimental synthesis to close the loop, so that a predicted property is confirmed in a real sample rather than left on paper. I am applying to engineering PhD programs in the United States for 2027 enrollment.
