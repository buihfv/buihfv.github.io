An ANSYS study asking whether combining two composite materials improves a fishing rod's elastic and structural performance over the conventional single-material design.

## Motivation

A fishing rod has to bend far without breaking, and it has to do that outdoors, where the temperature swing between a summer deck and a winter sea is large enough to matter. Rods are usually made from a single composite; pairing two raises the obvious question of whether the combination actually buys anything, or whether the mismatch at the interface costs more than it gains.

## Approach

- **Structural.** Modelled the rod in ANSYS and compared a two-composite layup against a conventional single-material rod.
- **Impact.** Varied the velocity of a steel ball striking the rod to find the load threshold at failure — a single number that makes the two designs directly comparable.
- **Thermal.** Evaluated the thermal expansion coefficients of the two composites across the temperature range a rod actually sees in marine conditions, to check the pairing does not come apart in the field.
